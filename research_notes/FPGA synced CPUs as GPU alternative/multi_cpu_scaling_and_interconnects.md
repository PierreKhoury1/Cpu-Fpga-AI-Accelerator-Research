# Multi-CPU LLM Inference Scaling and Interconnect Bottlenecks (state as of October 2026)

Scope: evaluates whether an FPGA that "syncs multiple CPUs together to act like a GPU" can overcome the compute and memory-bandwidth limits of the CPUs it connects. Research date: 2026-10-27. Every figure is dated to its source; "measured" vs "theoretical/vendor claim" vs "derived (my arithmetic)" is marked. Note: the shared web-search budget for this session was exhausted during the last round, so a few items (NDR/XDR adapter latency, a clean measured PCIe 5.0 x16 figure, Enfabrica EMFASYS specifications) could not be closed and are listed as gaps.

## Key Question 1: Bandwidth and latency of every link that can tie CPUs together (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Socket-to-socket coherent links (UPI 2.0 at 48 GB/s per direction per link; AMD xGMI at ~64 GB/s per direction per x16 link, up to 4 links, measured ~128 GB/s unidirectional on 3 links) are 5–30x faster and 10–100x lower latency (~200 ns remote-memory) than anything that leaves the board (PCIe 5.0 x16 63 GB/s theoretical per direction; CXL ~25–30 GB/s per x8 port at 140–735 ns load-to-use; RDMA ~0.5–3 µs; TCP tens of µs; Wi-Fi 3–7 ms), while NVLink 5 at 1.8 TB/s per GPU is another 6–40x above UPI/xGMI. The ranking by latency, not bandwidth, is what governs batch-1 decode.

### Inferences
- Ordered by one-way/round-trip latency (lowest first): UPI/xGMI remote memory (~200 ns measured, Genoa) → direct CXL (140–410 ns ASIC, 375–735 ns FPGA) → switched CXL (+250 ns) → RDMA/InfiniBand adapter (0.5–0.6 µs) and FPGA LTL RTT (2.9 µs) → kernel TCP on 10 GbE (sub-ms; 0.17 ms per-token RTT measured in one RPC setup) → Thunderbolt without RDMA (300 µs) → Wi-Fi (3–7 ms). Ordered by bandwidth per link (highest first): NVLink 5 (900 GB/s per direction per GPU) → PCIe 6.0 x16 (121 GB/s) → xGMI x16 (64 GB/s) / PCIe 5.0 x16 (63 GB/s) / UPI 2.0 (48 GB/s) → 400 GbE/NDR (50 GB/s) → CXL x8 (25–30 GB/s) → NUMAlink 8 (13.3 GB/s) → 100 GbE (12.5 GB/s) → 10 GbE (1.25 GB/s).
- Derived: six UPI 2.0 links give at most 288 GB/s per direction out of a Xeon 6900P socket, versus 900 GB/s per direction per Blackwell GPU over NVLink 5; AMD's four xGMI links give ~256 GB/s per direction. A 2-socket CPU box therefore has 3–4x less inter-chip bandwidth than one GPU-to-GPU NVLink connection, and ~1/40 of the 130 TB/s NVL72 switch fabric.
- FPGA-implemented links are slower than ASIC: the FPGA CXL path is 375–735 ns (2.9x DRAM) vs 2.2x for ASIC CXL, and the "Dissecting CXL" study found the FPGA-based expander lowest-performing. Any FPGA sync device therefore starts with a latency handicap relative to the ASIC NICs/switches it would replace.

- No clean measured PCIe 5.0 x16 host-device bandwidth figure (the only forum data point is anomalous); Wikipedia's 63 GB/s is theoretical.
- No measured Granite Rapids/Diamond Rapids cross-socket latency in ns; the Chips and Cheese figures are chart-only.
- No measured load-to-use latency for Versal Premium Gen 2 CXL 3.1 or Agilex CXL 2.0 products from vendor datasheets; only academic measurements of Agilex hard IP.

## Key Question 2: Measured distributed CPU-only (and consumer-cluster) LLM inference

### Takeaway
Every measured multi-node result found shows decode throughput scaling far below linear and often negative: llama.cpp RPC slows decode (72B: 11.1 → 5.9 tok/s over 10 GbE; Qwen3-235B: 20.4 → 15.2 tok/s from 1 to 4 Macs), distributed-llama's tensor parallelism gives 1.3–2x on 2–4 nodes before network time dominates at 8, Intel's own 8-socket tensor-parallel run reaches 2.85x over 2 sockets (71% efficiency), and the only above-linear-looking gains (exo with Thunderbolt-5 RDMA, 5 → 25 tok/s) came from cutting link latency 6x, not from adding bandwidth or nodes.

## Key Question 3: Communication volume and latency floor required by tensor- and pipeline-parallel inference

### Takeaway
For Megatron-style tensor parallelism a decode step needs two all-reduces per layer of a batch×hidden vector (16 KiB in bf16 for hidden 8192 at batch 1), i.e., 160 all-reduces per token for an 80-layer 70B model; the bytes are trivial (≤4.4 MiB per node per token) but the ~160–2240 sequential link traversals per token set a latency floor that exceeds the CPU memory-bandwidth floor only on TCP/Wi-Fi-class links or at large node counts, while on PCIe/CXL/RDMA-class links the CPUs' DRAM bandwidth (tens of ms per token for 70B) remains the binding limit.

### Cited Findings
- Pope et al. (Google, 2022): 1D weight-stationary tensor-parallel FFN needs "one all-gather and one reduce-scatter" per layer, T_comm = 2·B·L·E / network bandwidth (B batch, L sequence, E embed dim); 2D layout adds a second pair, T_comm = 8·B·L·E / (√n_chips × bandwidth); parallel attention/FFN layers remove one of two all-reduces; "at small batch sizes and sequence lengths, the time to load weights dominates"; PaLM 540B int8: 29 ms/token (28.5 ms at batch 64) on 64 TPU v4 chips; 76% MFU in prefill — [arXiv 2211.05102](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.05102v1)
- kipply, "Transformer Inference Arithmetic" (2022): memory-bound decode time = 2·P·b / (N·A_bm) (P params, N accelerators, A_bm bandwidth); 52B on 4× A100 (1.5e12 B/s) ≈ 17 ms/token; compute-bound above batch ≈ 312e12/1.5e12 ≈ 208 tokens on A100; tensor-parallel comms "four communications per layer", bytes per layer per token ≈ 4·(N−1)·d_model/N; latency term 4·n_layers·8 µs (≈2 ms for 64 layers); comms cannot be driven to zero by adding chips — [kipp.ly](https://kipp.ly/transformer-inference-arithmetic/)
- Horace He (2022): three regimes (compute, memory bandwidth, overhead); "if our bandwidth can't keep up then our performance isn't also going to double"; A100 312 TFLOPS vs 1.5 TB/s; non-matmul ops ~0.2% of BERT FLOPs but memory-bound — [horace.io](https://horace.io/brrr_intro.html)
- ATP (2023): TP all-reduce communication time scales with input [b, s, h] — [arXiv 2301.08658](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08658)
- UMD/YALIS "Beyond NCCL" (updated 2025-09-16): Llama-3.1-70B, batch 16, prefill 2480 / decode 2048, A100 nodes on Slingshot-11: "the prefill All-Reduce message size is 620 MiB ... the decode All-Reduce message size is 256 KiB"; at 32 GPUs communication is ">50% of the time" for both prefill and decode; their NVRAR all-reduce cut decode time-between-tokens up to 15% (70B, 16 GPUs) and 38.8% (405B, 64 GPUs) — [pssg.cs.umd.edu](https://pssg.cs.umd.edu/blog/2025/beyond-nccl/)
- NVIDIA (JAX/XLA blog): Gemma2 with 8-way TP on H100s, all-reduce ≈23% of end-to-end decode latency; ring all-reduce is bandwidth-optimal only above ~10 MB and adds up to ~2x latency for small messages; collectives could not overlap compute due to data dependencies — [NVIDIA developer blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/optimizing-for-low-latency-communication-in-inference-workloads-with-jax-and-xla)
- prima.cpp authors: dllama performs 64 all-reduces per token for an 8B (32-layer) model and 160 for 70B (80 layers), i.e., 2 per layer — [arXiv 2504.08791v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08791v2)
- distributed-llama measured sync traffic for Llama 3 8B (hidden 4096, 32 layers): Q80 buffer 544 kB/token on 2 devices, 1632 kB on 4, 3808 kB on 8 (root-node totals) — [distributed-llama README mirror](https://github.com/laujianmin/distributed-llama)
- Pipeline parallel per-token traffic: prima.cpp sends one FP32 embedding vector ("4e bytes") per layer-window hop; Petals (pipeline over internet) runs ~1 step/s and is sensitive to RTT (1.71 → 1.23 steps/s when RTT rises from <5 ms to 100 ms) — [arXiv 2504.08791v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08791v2); [arXiv 2209.01188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.01188)
- Catapult v2 measured FPGA-to-FPGA round trips: 2.88 µs (same TOR), 7.72 µs (L1), 18.71 µs (L2) — [Catapult v2 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Cloud-Scale-Acceleration-Architecture.pdf)
- SGLang on 2 sockets: shared-memory TP all-reduce overhead 3% of end-to-end (measured) — [LMSYS blog](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2025-07-14-intel-xeon-optimization)

### Inferences (worked numbers — all derived from the cited formulas and link figures; assumptions flagged)
Model assumptions: Llama-2/3-70B class, hidden E = 8192, 80 layers (per the assignment; consistent with prima.cpp's 160 all-reduces/token), Megatron TP with 2 all-reduces per layer → 160 all-reduces per token. Batch 1 decode message size per all-reduce: 8192 × 2 B = 16 KiB (bf16); 32 KiB (fp32); ~8.5 KiB (Q8).

1. Bytes per node per token (ring all-reduce moves 2(N−1)/N × S per node per collective; S = 16 KiB):
   - N = 2: 16 KiB × 160 = 2.5 MiB/token; N = 4: 24 KiB × 160 = 3.75 MiB; N = 8: 28 KiB × 160 = 4.4 MiB.
   - Bandwidth time for N = 8 (4.4 MiB): 1 GbE (0.125 GB/s) ≈ 37 ms; 10 GbE ≈ 3.7 ms; 100 GbE ≈ 0.37 ms; 400 GbE/NDR (50 GB/s) ≈ 0.09 ms; PCIe 5.0 x16 (63 GB/s) ≈ 0.07 ms; CXL x8 (25–30 GB/s) ≈ 0.16 ms; UPI 2.0 link (48 GB/s) ≈ 0.09 ms. Bandwidth is irrelevant above ~10 GbE at batch 1; it matters at batch 16+ (256 KiB messages per the UMD measurement → 16x these numbers) and in prefill (620 MiB messages).

2. Latency floor per token (sequential link traversals × per-traversal latency):
   - Ring: 2(N−1) steps per all-reduce → 320 (N=2), 960 (N=4), 2240 (N=8) steps per token.
   - At ~0.5 µs/step (UPI/xGMI shared memory, remote-memory ~200 ns plus sync): 0.16 / 0.5 / 1.1 ms. Consistent with SGLang's measured 3% overhead on 2 sockets.
   - At ~1–2 µs/step (PCIe DMA or CXL load/store hop; the CXL load-to-use of 0.4–0.7 ns-class figures plus doorbell/completion — the 1–2 µs is an assumption, not measured here): 0.3–0.6 / 1–2 / 2.2–4.5 ms.
   - At ~3 µs/step (RDMA incl. software, Catapult LTL RTT 2.88 µs): 1.0 / 2.9 / 6.7 ms.
   - At ~50 µs/step (kernel TCP on 10 GbE; assumption, consistent with the 0.17 ms per-token RPC RTT measured): 16 / 48 / 112 ms.
   - At 3–7 ms/step (Wi-Fi): 1–2.2 s (N=2) to 7–16 s (N=8); matches the ~24–30 s/token prima.cpp reports for dllama 70B on 4 Wi-Fi devices.
   - Tree/star reduction through a central FPGA: 2 hops per all-reduce regardless of N → 320 hops per token; at 2 µs/hop ≈ 0.64 ms/token, at 5 µs/hop ≈ 1.6 ms/token. This is the best case an FPGA sync device could offer: it converts the O(N) ring floor into an O(1) floor, but only if its host-to-FPGA hop is genuinely 1–2 µs (ACCL+ found the host-data path merely "comparable" to software RDMA MPI — see Key Question 4).

3. Memory-bandwidth floor per token (weights ÷ aggregate DRAM bandwidth), 70B at INT8 = 70 GB, Q4 ≈ 40 GB:
   - 2× Xeon 6980P with MRDIMM (1.45 TB/s measured effective): 48 ms (INT8), 28 ms (Q4); measured SGLang 70B INT8 TPOT 76.5 ms.
   - 4 sockets (≈2.9 TB/s): 24 / 14 ms; 8 sockets (≈5.8 TB/s): 12 / 7 ms — plus the latency floor above (2–7 ms for ring over PCIe/RDMA at N=8).
   - 2× Xeon 8563C-class (≈0.6 TB/s): 70B BF16 233 ms predicted vs 249.7 ms measured; 8 sockets 58 ms predicted vs 87.7 ms measured.
   - For comparison, a single A100 (1.5 TB/s HBM) is 47 ms (INT8 70B) and a TPU v4 chip (1.2 TB/s) 58 ms; the per-GPU NVLink-5 fabric (900 GB/s per direction) exceeds the DRAM bandwidth of any single CPU socket.
   - Conclusion: for N ≤ 8 CPUs on PCIe/CXL/RDMA-class links, per-token time ≈ max(memory floor, latency floor) ≈ memory floor; the interconnect is not the limiter, the CPUs' DRAM bandwidth is. Communication becomes the limiter only on TCP/Wi-Fi links (where it dominates by 10–1000x) or at N ≥ 16–32 on ring topologies (consistent with UMD's ">50% at 32 GPUs" even on NVLink/Slingshot).

4. Prefill vs decode: prefill moves 620 MiB per all-reduce (UMD, batch 16 × 2480 tokens) so it is bandwidth- and compute-bound; decode moves 16–256 KiB per all-reduce and is latency-bound (NVIDIA: ring adds ~2x latency below ~10 MB; kipply: fixed ~8 µs per message × 4 per layer). Pipeline parallelism at batch 1 keeps only one stage busy per token (Geerling's round-robin observation), so it needs ≥N in-flight requests/micro-batches to use N nodes.

### Gaps
- No source gives measured per-collective latency for CPU-side all-reduce over PCIe NTB or CXL; the 1–2 µs/hop figure is an assumption.
- No measured communication-time breakdown for Intel's 8-socket run; the ~30 ms overhead is inferred from the memory-bound model.
- Llama-70B hidden size / layer count were taken from the assignment, not re-verified against the model config.

## Key Question 4: What an FPGA could realistically do as interconnect / synchronization / collective device

### Takeaway
FPGAs have been deployed at scale as network-attached accelerators (Brainwave/Catapult: 2.9–23.5 µs FPGA-to-FPGA round trips, 35.9 TFLOPS at batch 1 with weights pinned on-chip) and as collective engines (ACCL+: 95 Gb/s, lower latency only when the data already lives in the FPGA; for CPU host data it is merely "comparable to software RDMA MPI while freeing CPU cycles"), so an FPGA can lower per-collective latency to a few µs and offload CPU cycles, but it adds zero DRAM bandwidth and zero FLOPs to the CPUs it connects, which is where the per-token time is spent.

### Cited Findings

FPGA collective offload / in-network reduction
- ACCL+ (ETH Zurich + AMD, OSDI 2024): 10 AMD Alveo U55C FPGAs, each with 100 Gb/s Ethernet, 10 EPYC CPUs each with 100 Gb/s Mellanox NICs, Cisco Nexus 9336C-FX2 switches; peak send/recv 95 Gbps; "ACCL+ exhibits significantly lower latency in F2F scenarios compared to software RDMA MPI for FPGA-generated data. In H2H scenarios, ACCL+ has comparable performance to software RDMA MPI for CPU-generated data while freeing up CPU cycles and reducing pressure on CPU caches"; for reduce and all-to-all on host data ACCL+ "shows only marginal benefits and, in some cases, falls short of software MPI"; Coyote invocation time "mainly consists of a PCIe write and a PCIe read latency"; distributed CPU vector-matrix multiply with ACCL+ reduction beat MPI (attributed to reduced CPU cache pressure); an 8-FPGA DLRM inference achieved >100x lower latency and >10x higher throughput than a 32-vCPU Cascade Lake TensorFlow Serving baseline — [ACCL+ paper (USENIX PDF)](https://usenix.org/system/files/osdi24-he.pdf)
- ACCL (H2RC 2021, "FPGA-Accelerated Collectives over 100 Gbps TCP-IP"): MPI-style collectives (bcast, scatter, gather, all-gather, reduce, all-reduce, reduce-scatter) as Vitis kernels over TCP/UDP at up to 100 Gbps; latency advantage claimed from bypassing the system NIC path — [ACCL docs](https://accl.readthedocs.io/); [ETH PDF](https://research-collection.ethz.ch/bitstream/handle/20.500.11850/510849/1/H2RC21_Xilinx_submitted_version.pdf); [GitHub Xilinx/ACCL](https://github.com/Xilinx/ACCL/blob/main/README.md)

### Inferences
- The realistic FPGA roles, in decreasing order of evidence: (a) network-attached accelerator that does the math itself with weights in on-chip SRAM (Brainwave — but note a 2018 Stratix 10 held ~47 MB of model, so a 70 GB 70B model would need ~1,500 such FPGAs); (b) SmartNIC/IPU offloading transport and crypto (AccelNet, IPU, U45N); (c) collective engine for data that already lives in FPGA memory (ACCL+ F2F); (d) in-network aggregation for large training all-reduces (NetReduce 1.5–1.7x). The specific proposal — an FPGA whose job is to synchronize CPUs — maps to ACCL+'s H2H mode, where the measured result is parity with software RDMA MPI, not a speedup.
- An FPGA sync device cannot change the two quantities that set CPU decode time: per-socket DRAM bandwidth (≈0.5–0.7 TB/s) and CPU matrix throughput. At best it holds the per-token latency floor to ~0.3–1.6 ms (320 hops at 1–5 µs) for any N, versus a 12–48 ms memory floor for 70B on 2–8 sockets — so it removes a term that was already small on PCIe/RDMA links. It would matter on 10 GbE TCP (16–112 ms floor) or Wi-Fi, i.e., it fixes a problem that an off-the-shelf RDMA NIC (0.5–3 µs) already fixes for ~$1–3k per node.
- Cost sanity: one Alveo U45N ($2.4–2.8k list) per node is similar in price to a 400G ConnectX-7-class NIC and adds 150 W; the Enfabrica precedent shows the market value of a fabric chip is in attaching GPUs to large memory pools (NVIDIA's >$900M deal), not in aggregating CPUs.
- FPGA-implemented links are 1.3–2x higher latency than ASIC links (CXL 2.9x vs 2.2x DRAM), so an FPGA in the data path is a latency cost unless the reduction logic it hosts saves more hops than it adds.

- No price for Intel IPU F2000X-PL; no public EMFASYS pricing or 2026 shipment confirmation; the NVIDIA–Enfabrica deal terms (license vs acquisition) are press reports only.
- No measured SHARPv4 small-message all-reduce latency; no FPGA-based all-reduce latency measured for 16 KiB messages specifically.

## Key Question 5: Precedents and lessons — scale-up shared-memory CPU machines, ScaleMP, Xeon Phi, and why many weak nodes lose

### Takeaway
Thirty-two-socket NUMAlink machines (HPE Superdome Flex) exist, but their inter-chassis links (13.3 GB/s per port) are a quarter of one UPI 2.0 link, their aggregate DRAM bandwidth is just the sum of sockets, and an 8-socket system cost ~$2.5M in a 2022 TPC-H disclosure; Xeon Phi was discontinued in 2018 for lack of demand against GPUs; and the arithmetic (per-token time = weights ÷ aggregate bandwidth + per-collective latency × collectives) means N weak nodes beat one fast chip only if the sum of their memory bandwidths exceeds its HBM and their link latency × 160–2240 hops stays below its decode time, which no CPU cluster found in this research achieves.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Why shared-memory giants are not AI machines (derived): a 32-socket Superdome Flex with ~0.3–0.5 TB/s per socket would total ~10–16 TB/s of DRAM bandwidth only if every socket streamed its own local weights with zero remote traffic; its 853 GB/s bisection is below the HBM bandwidth of a single modern GPU (A100 1.5 TB/s; NVLink 5 fabric 130 TB/s per NVL72), and its 2022 8-socket price (~$2.5M incl. storage) buys many GPU nodes. The coherence fabric is optimized for cache-line-granularity transactional workloads (SAP HANA), not for the streaming matrix traffic of inference.
- The general argument (Amdahl + communication-to-compute ratio, from Pope/kipply): decode time per token ≈ max(W/(N·BW_node), (hops × L_link)) where W is the weight bytes. Adding nodes shrinks the first term as 1/N but the second grows with N (ring) or stays constant (tree). For CPUs, BW_node ≈ 0.5 TB/s vs HBM 1.5–8 TB/s per GPU, so it takes 3–16 CPU sockets to match one GPU's weight-streaming rate, by which point the latency term (2–7 ms at N=8 over RDMA) is of the same order as a GPU's whole decode step (kipply: 17 ms for 52B on 4 A100; Pope: 29 ms for 540B on 64 TPUs). Prefill is worse: CPU AMX/AVX throughput per socket is orders of magnitude below GPU tensor-core throughput (A100 312 TFLOPS), so the compute term, not communication, dominates — an interconnect cannot fix that either.
- Lesson from ScaleMP and Xeon Phi: software/fabric glue that makes many x86 parts look like one bigger x86 part sells at a 25% premium into niches but does not displace a purpose-built accelerator whose advantage is on-package memory bandwidth and matrix throughput; Intel itself folded Phi's ideas back into Xeon rather than scale the node count.
