# FPGAs as AI Accelerators and CPU-Attached Coprocessors (state of market, October 2026)

Scope note: all prices in USD unless stated; every figure is dated. "Vendor claim" = taken from a vendor datasheet/press release; "independent/measured" = academic paper, forum measurement, or third-party benchmark. Where a figure is my own arithmetic from cited inputs it is labelled "(my arithmetic)".

## KQ1. Current FPGA AI silicon: peak INT8 TOPS, memory bandwidth, board price, power

### Takeaway
The biggest AI-oriented FPGAs today land at roughly 100–400 peak INT8 TOPS (AMD Versal AI Engines) or ~60–90 INT8 TOPS (Altera Agilex 7 / Achronix fabric DSP), with HBM2e boards at ~820 GB/s and $9.5k–$15k list price; measured kernel efficiency on AMD's AI Engines is ~60–70% of peak, and the one card that combined 404 INT8 TOPS with a data-center form factor (Alveo V70) was discontinued. Note the structural split in AMD's line-up: the HBM parts (Versal HBM / Alveo V80) are sold as networking/HPC cards, while the AI Engine parts (AI Core / AI Edge) ship with LPDDR4 or DDR4 only.

### Inferences
- The only current FPGA boards with >100 INT8 TOPS in a PCIe form factor are AMD AI-Engine parts (V70 — now discontinued — and the VCK5000 based on VC1902); the HBM boards (V80, Versal HBM, Agilex 7 M-series) rely on fabric DSP for compute (~60–90 INT8 TOPS theoretical) and are marketed for networking/HPC, so there is no single off-the-shelf card that pairs ~400 TOPS AI Engines with ~800 GB/s HBM.
- Independent measurements consistently land at 60–70% of vendor peak for well-tuned GEMM kernels (MaxEVA 68%, Myrtle 60%) and far lower for end-to-end models (CHARM: 1–16% of VC1902 peak), so a realistic planning number for an AI-Engine FPGA is 30–80 INT8 TOPS sustained.
- Lead times (8–33 weeks on AMD/Altera kits in Oct 2026) and the V70 discontinuation indicate low-volume, long-cycle supply rather than a commodity accelerator market.

- Lattice CertusPro-NX AI numbers not found; Microchip VectorBlox TOPS/W not published.

## KQ2. Software stacks and engineering effort (Vitis AI/DPU, FINN, hls4ml, FPGA AI Suite, HLS)

### Takeaway
The push-button stacks (Vitis AI DPU, Altera FPGA AI Suite/OpenVINO, FINN, hls4ml) work well for CNNs and small quantized nets but each is tied to specific devices and has been pruned (Vitis AI 3.5 dropped Alveo U50/U280/VCK5000; Vitis 2024.1 dropped U280 platforms; V80 ships with a Vivado-only RTL flow); nobody publishes month-level effort numbers, but the academic LLM accelerators below are each multi-person research projects, and Microsoft's own retrospective says "lack of common shell, tools makes portability difficult; 3rd party programs largely failed."

### Cited Findings
- Vitis AI DPUCZDX8G (Zynq UltraScale+/Kria) B4096 configuration = 4,096 ops/cycle (pixel/in-channel/out-channel parallelism 8/16/16); TOPS depends on clock (4096 x 300 MHz ≈ 1.2 TOPS, my arithmetic; a KR260 benchmark ran mostly at 150 MHz) — [AMD PG338](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/pg338-dpu/Architecture-of-the-DPUCZDX8G); [Hackster KR260 DPU benchmark](https://hackster.io/iotengineer22/benchmark-architectures-of-the-dpu-with-kr260-699f19)
- Vitis AI DPUCVDX8G (Versal AI Engine DPU): peak 10.24 TOPS (C32B1CU1L2S2) to 102.4 TOPS (C64B5CU1L2S2) at 333 MHz PL / 1.25 GHz AIE — [AMD PG389](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/pg389-dpucvdx8g/Peak-Theoretical-Performance-Metrics)
- KV260 with DPUCZDX8G: ResNet-50 ≈62 fps, MobileNet v1 187 fps, MobileNet v2 169 fps (third-party, single data point); default KV260 image uses B4096; models must be compiled for the exact DPU architecture loaded — [Medium write-up](https://medium.com/p/576a2427b3b7); [Pixela Vitis AI guide](https://www15.pixela.co.jp/products/pickup/dev/ai/vitisai_ai_3_model_zoo_en.html)
- Vitis AI Library 3.5 release notes: "Alveo U50 and U280 cards are no longer supported. Use Vitis AI 1.4.x if using these cards"; VCK5000-Prod dropped (use Vitis AI 3.0) — [AMD UG1354 v3.5 release notes](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1354-xilinx-ai-sdk/Vitis-AI-Library-v3.5-Release-Notes)
- XRT/Vitis 2024.1 removed support for Alveo U50LV, U55N and U280 XDMA platforms; a university cluster notes "Vitis 24.1 available, but no longer supports Alveo U280 target" — [AMD UG1451 XRT 2024.1](https://docs.amd.com/r/2024.1-English/ug1451-xrt-release-notes/New-Features-and-Changes); [Paderborn PC2 wiki](https://upb-pc2.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/PC2DOK/pages/1903116)
- Alveo V80 flow: AMD datasheet describes the HBM2e bandwidth as "available via the AMD Vivado Design Suite with a flow that is streamlined for hardware designers"; the card ships with AVED example design for Vivado — [AMD V80 page](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/alveo/v80/a-v80-p64g-pq-g.html); [Farnell V80 datasheet](https://www.farnell.com/datasheets/4218302.pdf)
- FINN ResNet-50 on Alveo U250 (W1A2, 4-bit residuals, 8-bit first/last layers): 2,000 fps max, 2 ms batch-1 latency, 70 W at max fps, 65% top-1 / 85% top-5 ImageNet — [PYNQ forum, FINN team](https://discuss.pynq.io/t/resnet50-dataflow-inference-with-pynq-on-alveo/1003); [finn-examples PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/finn-examples/0.0.5/)
- BARVINN comparison table lists FINN-R ResNet-50 (1/2-bit) at 178 MHz, 2,873 fps, and notes the fine-tuned ResNet-50 "requires more than 87% of Alveo U250 accelerator's resources" — [BARVINN, arXiv 2301.00290](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.00290)
- hls4ml: 2018 paper argues translating a network "takes minutes to hours" rather than designing a bespoke architecture; later work says balancing performance/resources requires "iterative, per-layer manual tuning of reuse factors" and that the standard flow leaves engineers to write firmware and do HW/SW integration, verification and debugging, "an onerous task" — [hls4ml 2018, arXiv 1804.06913](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06913); [De-specializing hls4ml, arXiv 2103.13060](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.13060); [CGRA4ML, arXiv 2408.15561](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.15561)
- Altera FPGA AI Suite + OpenVINO: push-button flow from TensorFlow/PyTorch to bitstream on Agilex 3/5/7; Altera's model-performance tables are generated with dla_build_example_design.py at -2 speed grade, Agilex 5 at 350 MHz and Agilex 7 at 400 MHz, and the "IP Throughput" column covers only the FPGA-resident part of the graph — [Intel FPGA AI Suite model performance 2025.3](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/programmable/863373/2025-3/model-performance.html); [eeNews Europe Agilex 3](https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/altera-details-agilex-3-embedded-ai-fpga)
- FINN "excels at very low bitwidths"; hls4ml is "the most popular tool in the scientific computing community because it is user-friendly" — [CGRA4ML, arXiv 2408.15561](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.15561)
- Microsoft (Putnam, NANDA 2024 keynote) on cloud FPGA programs: "Customers don't actually want you to differentiate; Lack of common shell, tools, makes portability difficult; 3rd party programs largely failed" — [Putnam NANDA 2024 keynote PDF](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~phjk/NANDA24/Slides/Catapult_Putnam_NANDA_2024_Keynote_Public_pdf.pdf)
- Positron (vendor): Atlas runs Hugging Face .pt/.safetensors transformer models "without a custom compiler"; first customer went "from unboxing to running LLMs in 38 minutes" — this is for a finished appliance, not for building an accelerator — [BittWare-Molex Positron Atlas page](https://www.bittware-molex.com/products/positron-atlas/)

### Inferences
- A CNN on a supported Kria/Versal board with Vitis AI or FPGA AI Suite is a days-to-weeks task (quantize, compile, deploy) because the DPU bitstream is pre-built; a transformer/LLM is not covered by any vendor push-button flow on FPGA, so the realistic path is custom RTL/HLS (the FlightLLM/DFX/EdgeLLM class of work), which in the literature is a multi-author, year-scale research project.
- Toolchain churn is a first-order risk: in three years AMD dropped Vitis AI and Vitis platform support for the exact boards (U50/U280/VCK5000) used by the best-known LLM-on-FPGA papers, and its newest data-center card (V80) ships with an RTL-only flow.

### Gaps
- No source gives month-level engineering effort or cost for porting a CNN or transformer to an FPGA; no salary/rate data for RTL/HLS engineers was collected.
- No explicit AMD statement that Vitis (as opposed to Vivado) is unsupported on V80 — only that the shipped flow is Vivado/AVED.

## KQ3. Best published LLM / transformer inference on FPGA (2022–2026)

### Takeaway
The best single-FPGA LLM results on 7B-class models are ~55 tokens/s (Alveo U280) and ~92 tokens/s (Versal VHK158) at batch 1 with ~3.5-bit weights (FlightLLM, 2024) and ~67 tokens/s for ChatGLM2-6B on a VCU128 (EdgeLLM, 2025); these beat a V100S/A100 running the same batch-1 workload on energy efficiency (4–7x) but not on absolute throughput vs. an optimized A100 (gpt-fast INT4: 197 tokens/s). Positron's Agilex-7M appliance claims ~2x two H100s on Llama 3.1 8B at one-third the power, but no independent benchmark exists.

### Cited Findings
- **FlightLLM (ISFPGA 2024)**: LLaMA2-7B and OPT-6.7B, batch 1, weights mixed 3/4/5-bit (avg 3.5 b), activations 8-bit, softmax/LN in fp16; U280 kernel at 225 MHz. Throughput ≈55 tokens/s on U280 (from figure) and 92.5 tokens/s on VHK158; gpt-fast on A100 INT4 = 196.8 tokens/s. Latency 1.2–1.6x better than V100S; energy efficiency (tokens/J) on U280: 6.0x vs V100S-naive, 4.4x vs A100-naive, 3.8x vs A100-opt (LLaMA2-7B); VHK158 2.9x vs gpt-fast/A100; cost efficiency 1.4–2.3x using V100S≈$12k, A100≈$17k, U280≈$8k; HBM bandwidth utilization U280 65.9%, VHK158 64.8% vs A100-opt 57.4%. Absolute power not reported in text — [FlightLLM, arXiv 2401.03868v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.03868v2); [SJTU PDF](https://dai.sjtu.edu.cn/my_file/pdf/94c37d8a-7f86-4f95-ae72-05a79da5bb61.pdf)
- **DFX (MICRO 2022, KAIST/NAVER)**: GPT-2 (345M/774M/1.5B) on 4x Alveo U280; 5.58x speedup, 3.99x energy efficiency, 8.21x cost-effectiveness vs 4x V100; 3.20x/4.46x/5.58x for 1/2/4 FPGAs; a 2024 survey quotes ~120 tokens/s for GPT-2 1.5B (unverified against the paper) — [DFX arXiv 2209.10797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10797v1); [Hot Chips 34 poster](https://hc34.hotchips.org/assets/program/posters/HC2022.KAIST.SeongminHong.v03.pdf); [survey arXiv 2401.09890](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.09890)
- **EdgeLLM (TCAS-I 2025; arXiv Jul 2024)**: CPU-FPGA heterogeneous design on AMD VCU128; ChatGLM2-6B average 67 tokens/s at 55.07 W; Qwen-7B 42.5–69.4 tokens/s; claims 1.91x throughput and 7.55x energy efficiency vs A100-SXM4-80G and 10–24% better than FlightLLM — [EdgeLLM arXiv 2407.21325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.21325); [survey arXiv 2410.04466](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.04466)

### Inferences
- All credible FPGA LLM wins are batch-1, low-bit (≤4-bit weights), memory-bandwidth-bound decode scenarios where the FPGA's higher HBM utilization (65–93% claimed) offsets its 5–10x lower peak compute; at batch >1 or with optimized GPU kernels (gpt-fast INT4 on A100 at 197 tok/s), the GPU retakes absolute throughput.
- Per-card decode throughput scales with memory bandwidth: U280 (460 GB/s) ≈55 tok/s and VHK158 (819 GB/s) ≈92 tok/s for 7B at ~3.5 bits is consistent with ~65% bandwidth utilization (my arithmetic: 7B x 3.5 b ≈ 3.1 GB/token → 460 GB/s x 0.66 / 3.1 GB ≈ 98 tok/s upper bound on U280; the measured 55 includes activation/KV traffic and overhead).
- Positron's own trajectory (FPGA Atlas → Asimov ASIC) is the clearest 2026 datapoint that FPGA LLM inference is a stepping stone to an ASIC, not an end state.

## KQ4. History and lessons: Brainwave/Catapult, Intel–Altera, Xilinx/AMD, Baidu/Alibaba

### Takeaway
Microsoft ran the "largest deployment of AI FPGAs" (Brainwave on Stratix 10: 39.5 TFLOPS ms-fp8 at batch 1, <1 ms latency, serving Bing/Azure), yet its own 2024 retrospective concludes Brainwave was "competitive (but generally inferior) on performance metrics compared to GPUs," that "FPGAs didn't win the HW lottery," and that Microsoft's FPGAs now live in Azure Boost networking/storage offload while inference moved to GPUs and the Maia ASIC line (Maia 200, Jan 2026). ### Inferences
- The Brainwave story is the strongest precedent: even with hyperscaler-level investment, a custom numeric format (ms-fp8) and the best batch-1 latency in the industry, the FPGA path was abandoned for inference because software optimization erodes accelerator speedups (250x→30x in months), models changed faster than FPGA hardware could be re-tuned, and GPUs/ASICs won on ecosystem — exactly the failure modes a new FPGA-based neural processor must plan around.

## KQ5. FPGA as CPU coprocessor / interconnect: bandwidth to host memory vs. local memory

### Takeaway
An FPGA cannot read a CPU's DRAM at anything near the CPU's own bandwidth: the best vendor-reported PCIe Gen5 x16 DMA is ~60 GB/s per direction (theoretical 64 GB/s), the only in-package coherent link ever shipped (Stratix 10 DX UPI) was 28 GB/s theoretical, HARP measured ~19 GB/s, and a CXL Type-2 FPGA sees ~590–620 ns per uncached 64-byte host read — versus 576 GB/s theoretical per EPYC 9005 socket and ~820 GB/s from the FPGA's own HBM2e. In other words, host-DRAM access is ~10% of the socket's bandwidth and ~7% of the FPGA's local HBM bandwidth.

### Cited Findings
- Intel Stratix 10 DX (2019): "first, and so far only, FPGAs designed to support Intel UPI"; UPI theoretical peak 28 GB/s at 11.2 GT/s; Intel estimates 37% lower worst-case round-trip latency than PCIe; PCIe Gen4 x16 theoretical 32 GB/s — [Guru3D](https://www.guru3d.com/news-story/intel-ships-stratix-10-dx-fpgasvmware-among-early-partners.html); [insideHPC](https://insidehpc.com/2019/09/intel-ships-stratix-10-dx-fpgas/)
- HARP (Xeon + Arria 10 in-package, one QPI coherent channel + two PCIe channels): FPGA read/write bandwidth to shared memory ≈19 GB/s (2019 TU Crete thesis); UCLA VAST microbenchmarks characterize CCI-P coherent cache hit/miss latency and remote memory bandwidth (DAC 2016) — [TU Crete thesis](https://dias.library.tuc.gr/view/83229); [UCLA VAST](https://vast.cs.ucla.edu/node/598)
- PCIe Gen3 x16 FPGA DMA: ≈14 GB/s read and write measured on Stratix 10 MX (Intel AN 881) vs 16 GB/s theoretical — [Intel AN 881](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/programmable/683291.html)
- PCIe Gen4 x16 FPGA DMA (Agilex, user report): 27.5 GB/s vs 32 GB/s theoretical; Intel says >29 GB/s write-only with 512 B MPS — [Altera community thread](https://community.altera.com/discussions/fpga-device/pcie-gen-4---16-lanes-in-agilex-maximum-rate/264791)
- PCIe Gen5 x16 FPGA DMA (vendor-reported, Atomic Rules Arkville): 50 GB/s each direction on Agilex 7 (Jan 2023); "60+60 GB/s" (Arkville 23.11, Nov 2023); 60 GB/s each direction on AMD Versal Gen 1, tested on Alveo V80 (Arkville 24.11, Nov 2024) — [BusinessWire Jan 2023](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230110005366/en/Atomic-Rules-Introduces-the-Worlds-Highest-Performance-PCIe-5.0-DMA-for-Intel-Agilex-7-FPGAs); [BusinessWire Nov 2023](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20231113775666/en/Atomic-Rules-Ships-Arkville%C2%AE-23.11-the-Trusted-and-Performant-Solution-for-FPGA-PCIe-DMA); [BusinessWire Nov 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241118580904/en/Atomic-Rules-Ships-Arkville%C2%AE-24.11-Adding-PCIe-Gen5-DMA-to-AMD-Versal%E2%84%A2-Gen-1-Devices)
- Alveo XDMA host interface is specified as Gen3 x16 with 512-bit datapath; Alveo U50 supports Gen4 only at x8 — [AMD UG1120](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1120-alveo-platforms/DMA-Configurations); [AMD DS965 U50](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ds965-u50/PCIe-Connector/Data-Rates)
- CXL on FPGAs: Agilex 7 I/M-series R-Tile has hard IP for PCIe 5.0 x16 and CXL Type 1/2/3 ("CXL runs on the same high-bandwidth interface as PCIe 5.0"); Intel forum: D2H write bandwidth on Agilex 7 I-Series CXL IP is link-limited and does not scale with a second CAFU port — [BittWare CXL](https://www.bittware.com/ip-solutions/cxl/); [Altera community CXL thread](https://community.altera.com/tag/cxl)
- Measured CXL characteristics (arXiv 2411.02814, Nov 2024; Intel Agilex 7 I-series Type-2 prototype on Sapphire/Emerald Rapids): Type-2 device non-cacheable read of host memory ≈590 ns (local NUMA) / 620 ns (remote), dropping to ≈110 ns on device-cache hit (256 KB host-cache with 2 DCOH slices); FPGA-based CXL memory ≈400 ns local load latency vs ASIC CXL 200–300 ns vs DIMM; FPGA-CXL minimum latency 1.52x (local) / 1.36x (remote) higher than ASIC CXL; CXL memory latency is 50–300% above DIMM; DDR5 DIMM max loaded latency 254–259 ns local; GH200 NVLink-C2C saturates at 125 GiB/s load / 149 GiB/s store vs 450 GB/s theoretical — [Hitchhiker's Guide to CXL/NVLink-C2C/Infinity Fabric, arXiv 2411.02814](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02814)
- Host CPU DRAM bandwidth reference: AMD EPYC 9005 — 12 DDR5 channels at 6000 MT/s, 576 GB/s theoretical per socket — [Supermicro EPYC spec table](https://supermicro.com/de/support/resources/cpu-amd-epyc-9004-7003?mlg=4); [AMD EPYC 9005 architecture overview](https://www.amd.com/content/dam/amd/en/documents/epyc-technical-docs/user-guides/58462_amd-epyc-9005-tg-architecture-overview.pdf)
- FPGA local memory references: Alveo V80 / Versal HBM 819 GB/s; Agilex 7 M-series HBM2e 820 GB/s, NoC up to 1 TB/s; U55C 460 GB/s — see KQ1 citations ([AMD V80](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/alveo/v80/a-v80-p64g-pq-g.html); [Intel Agilex 7 HBM2e](https://intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/programmable/683458/current/heterogeneous-3d-stacked-hbm2e-dram.html))
- Microsoft's Catapult/Brainwave used a "bump-in-the-wire" topology (FPGA between NIC and ToR switch, RDMA transport for AI networks) rather than host-memory bandwidth — [Putnam NANDA 2024 keynote](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~phjk/NANDA24/Slides/Catapult_Putnam_NANDA_2024_Keynote_Public_pdf.pdf)

### Inferences
- Ratio summary (my arithmetic from cited figures): FPGA→host DRAM ≈60 GB/s (Gen5 x16 DMA) vs 576 GB/s per EPYC socket = ~10%; vs FPGA local HBM2e 819 GB/s = ~7%; a four-socket "FPGA fed by CPUs" topology would still cap at ~4 x 60 = 240 GB/s even with one Gen5 x16 link per socket, below one HBM2e FPGA and ~14x below an H100's HBM3 (3.35 TB/s, NVIDIA published figure not fetched here).
- CXL does not change the bandwidth ceiling (same Gen5 x16 PHY) and adds ~2x latency vs DIMM; its benefit is coherence/pointer semantics, not throughput — so an FPGA "neural processor" whose weights live in CPU DRAM will be link-bound at ≤60 GB/s per socket.
- Fine-grained CPU↔FPGA synchronization is latency-bound at ~0.6 µs per uncached 64 B access on CXL Type-2 FPGA prototypes (vs ~0.1 µs cached), i.e., roughly 10x worse than on-package ASIC fabrics.

### Gaps
- No independent measurement of CXL Type-2 streaming bandwidth (GB/s) from an FPGA into host DRAM was found; only latency and the link-limited forum note.
- No measured UPI bandwidth for Stratix 10 DX (only Intel's theoretical 28 GB/s); the DAC 2016 HARP microbenchmark numbers were not retrieved.
- AMD "Alveo + EPYC" or Infinity-Fabric-attached FPGA products: no sources found (no such product appears to exist publicly).

## KQ6. Economics: cost per TOPS and per GB/s vs GPU; FPGA-vs-ASIC gap; low-cost boards

### Takeaway
On list prices, AMD's AI-Engine kits cost roughly $40–$100 per peak INT8 TOPS (VEK280 $6,995/179 TOPS; VCK190 $13,195/133 TOPS) versus ~$15 per dense INT8 TOPS for an H100 (~$30k/1,979 TOPS) and ~$2–3 for an RTX 5090, and HBM-equipped FPGA cards cost ~$10–12 per GB/s (U55C, V80); the canonical Kuon & Rose measurements put soft-logic FPGA at ~35x the area, 3–4x the delay and ~14x the dynamic power of a standard-cell ASIC (area gap ~18x with hard DSP/BRAM, as low as 4.7x when heavily used). ### Inferences
- $/peak-INT8-TOPS (my arithmetic, list prices): VEK280 ≈$39; VCK190 ≈$99; V70 unknown (discontinued); H100 ≈$12–18 (dense, at $25–35k); RTX 5090 ≈$2–3 (dense ~838 TOPS if 1,677 is sparse). Even before the 30–70% sustained-efficiency haircut, AI-Engine FPGAs are ~3–8x worse than H100 and ~15–40x worse than a consumer GPU per peak TOPS.
- $/(GB/s) (my arithmetic): U55C ≈$10.3; V80 ≈$11.6; VHK158 ≈$18.3; H100 ≈$7.5–10.4 at 3.35 TB/s (NVIDIA figure, not fetched) — FPGAs are roughly at parity-to-2x worse on memory-bandwidth cost, which is why bandwidth-bound batch-1 decode is the one place they look competitive.
- Kuon & Rose's 5–10x "rule of thumb" is conservative for DSP-heavy datapaths (area gap 4.7–18x with hard blocks, power ~14x) and does not include the clock-frequency gap (FPGA fabric 225–500 MHz in the cited designs vs. 1–2 GHz ASIC/GPU), so a soft-logic neural processor on FPGA should be budgeted at ≥10x worse perf/W than an equivalent ASIC.

### Gaps
- No single current-year source lists an H100/B200 street price together with dense INT8 TOPS; GPU prices above are from undated aggregator pages.
- No USD list price for Altera dev kits/cards from a US distributor (EUR/regional only); no price for Achronix VectorPath or BittWare IA-860m.
- No used-market (eBay sold) prices for Alveo cards could be retrieved.
